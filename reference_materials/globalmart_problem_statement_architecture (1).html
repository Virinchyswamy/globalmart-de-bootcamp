GlobalMart — Problem Statement & Architecture

Azure Databricks  |  3 Weeks  |  13 Days  |  2 Assessments  |  Simple-language version for session delivery

1. The Story

GlobalMart started as a small e-commerce player and grew fast — both in physical retail stores and online. As it grew, every team started asking the same question: "What does the data say?" But getting a straight answer became harder and harder.

Different teams pulled numbers from different places — and the numbers didn't match. Stakeholders stopped trusting reports. Decisions slowed down. What was supposed to make GlobalMart faster and smarter was instead creating friction.

2. The 5 Problems

1
Data Silos
No single source of truth. Orders in one system, products/customers in files dropped into storage.
2
Inconsistency & Quality
Same customer, three spellings. Missing values. Numbers that don't reconcile.
3
Access Control & Compliance
No clear rules on who should see what. Either everyone sees everything, or no one sees what they need.
4
Complex Transformations
Every team writes their own scripts to clean the same data, over and over.
5
Unclear Origin & Redundancy
Nobody can say where a number came from or when it was refreshed. Same data copied five places.
Result: nobody trusts the data, so nobody uses it the way it was meant to be used.

3. Why Databricks?

Before

Data was shattered across separate tools — every handoff was a chance for something to break or go stale.

  • One tool to store files
  • A different tool to schedule jobs
  • Another tool just for reporting
  • Manual email alerts for anything operational
  • No shared rulebook on access
After

Databricks replaces the scattered toolbox with one platform that does it all, on the same copy of the data.

  • Ingestion (Lakeflow Connect + Autoloader)
  • Storage & transformation (Delta Lake, Bronze/Silver/Gold)
  • Scheduling (Workflows)
  • Governance (Unity Catalog)
  • Reporting & self-serve Q&A (Genie)

fact_sales is the deliverable downstream teams actually asked for — one trusted table answering "what sold, when, to whom, for how much." Everything upstream (CDC, Autoloader, Bronze, Silver, dimensional model) exists to make this one table correct and current. Workflows keeps it refreshed on schedule, Unity Catalog governs who can see it, and Genie is what replaces the old "manual email alert" — a stakeholder asks a question and gets a trusted answer immediately.

5. fact_sales — The Business Requirement

Why this table is the point of the whole pipeline

Grain: one row per order line item

Measures:
  • quantity
  • unit_price
  • line_total
Connects to:
  • dim_customer — who bought it
  • dim_product — what was sold
  • dim_date — when
  • dim_address — where it shipped
  • dim_payment_method — how it was paid for
Answers the business problem:
  • Single source of truth for "what sold" (fixes Problem #1)
  • Built on validated Silver data (fixes Problem #2)
  • Governed access via Unity Catalog (fixes Problem #3)
  • Refreshed automatically by Workflows (fixes Problem #4)
  • Lineage traces every number back to its source (fixes Problem #5)
Same idea as last year's "Inventory Mail Alerts → DC/Warehouse Manager" — except now it's a governed, always-current table that Genie can answer questions from directly, not a one-off email.

6. The 13-Day Journey

Bring the data in (Lakeflow Connect + Autoloader)  →  clean and validate it (Silver)  →  model it properly (star schema, so fact_sales makes business sense)  →  build Gold  →  make it refresh automatically as new data arrives (incremental + SCD)  →  automate and monitor the whole pipeline (Workflows)  →  lock it down with proper access control (Unity Catalog)  →  let stakeholders ask questions of it directly (Genie).

Continuity note: same GlobalMart story and 5 problems as last year. What's new this year: Postgres CDC formalized via Lakeflow Connect, a dedicated dimensional-modeling day, incremental loading + SCD patterns, full Workflows orchestration, and Genie as the trust-closing deliverable — all in service of one governed fact_sales table.
