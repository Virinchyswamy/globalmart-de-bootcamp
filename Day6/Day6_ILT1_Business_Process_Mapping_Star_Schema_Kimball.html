1. What Is a Business Process?

A business process is a repeatable, measurable activity that happens in the business — something that generates events you can count, sum, or track over time. "Sales performance" is not a business process (too vague). A sale happening — a customer buying N units of a product, at a price, on a date — is.

Kimball's core rule: build the warehouse one business process at a time, not one report at a time. Model the report every team asks for as a fresh table, and you get GlobalMart's original Data Silos and Unclear Origin problems all over again. Model the sale process once, correctly, as fact_sales — every report becomes a different GROUP BY on the same trusted table.

Not a business processA business process
"Sales performance" — vague, no clear eventA sale happening — customer buys product(s), in an order, on a date
"Customer satisfaction" — an opinionA return being filed — a measurable event with date, product, reason
"Marketing effectiveness" — a judgmentA campaign being sent and opened — a measurable event

2. Facts vs. Dimensions

Kimball's one-liner: facts are verbs, dimensions are nouns. A sale happens (verb, fact) to a customer, of a product, on a date, shipped to an address, paid by a method, as part of an order (all nouns, dimensions).

FactDimension
What it representsA measurable business eventContext describing that event
ContainsNumbers to SUM/AVG/COUNT + foreign keysDescriptive attributes to filter/group by
GrowsFast — one row per eventSlowly — a customer doesn't change daily
GlobalMart examplefact_sales — one row per order line itemdim_customer, dim_product, dim_date, dim_address, dim_payment_method, dim_orders
Neither is useful alone. fact_sales with no dimensions is a pile of numbers — "₹14,392,000 in Sales_amount" means nothing without knowing whose sales, which products, when. dim_customer with no fact table is just a phone book. The value is in the join.

3. GlobalMart's Business Process

GlobalMart's core business process is: a customer buys product(s) in an order. Every ingestion pipeline, every Bronze/Silver table, every CDC and Autoloader job built so far exists to make this one process measurable correctly. Right now that data is clean in Silver, but scattered across 7 tables (orders, order_items, products, customers, addresses, payments, payment_methods) shaped like the source systems. The star schema's job: collapse it into one fact table surrounded by the dimensions that give it meaning.

fact_sales's grain, measures (Quantity_purchased, Actual_price, Discounted_price, Sales_amount), and 6 dimensions were already named back on Day 1 as this course's central deliverable. Today's job is to understand why that shape is the right one — grain and dimension design get their full rigorous treatment in ILT 2, right after this session.

dim_customerWho bought itOne row per customer version (SCD2)
dim_productWhat was soldOne row per product version (SCD2)
dim_dateWhenOne row per calendar day — generated, not sourced from Silver
dim_addressWhere it shippedOne row per address
dim_payment_methodHow it was paidOne row per payment method (~5 rows total)
dim_ordersOrder header contextOne row per order (channel, shipping tier, supplier)
Six dimensions — matching the Day 1 spec's spirit, with one addition: dim_orders for order-header attributes. ILT 2 (next) designs each one in depth: natural key, surrogate key, and which ones are realistic candidates for SCD Type 2 (history tracking). Two of them — dim_customer and dim_product — actually already carry that history by the time you get to them, because Silver (Day 5) built it in; today's hands-on republishes it in Gold, it doesn't build it from scratch.
